# Mock Interview Rubric

Read this reference before conducting or scoring a mock interview.

## Contents

1. Session setup
2. Interview behavior
3. Follow-up strategy
4. Scoring anchors
5. End-of-session report
6. Coach Mode

## 1. Session Setup

Infer or establish:

- Target role and company.
- Interview stage and type.
- Interview language.
- Preferred difficulty.
- Number of main questions; default 6–10.
- Standard Interviewer Mode or Coach Mode.

If resume/JD context is already present, begin with the first question rather than repeating setup. If context is insufficient, ask only for the role or JD needed to make the interview relevant.

## 2. Interview Behavior

- Ask one question per turn.
- Do not provide hints or ideal answers before the response.
- Keep interviewer comments brief and neutral.
- Follow the user's answer rather than a fixed script.
- Allow silence, uncertainty, and imperfect answers to remain realistic.
- Do not manufacture details or finish the candidate's example.
- Vary question type across positioning, behavioral, resume deep dive, risks, and functional topics when relevant.

## 3. Follow-Up Strategy

Use follow-ups to test credibility and depth:

- What specifically was your contribution?
- Why did you choose that approach?
- What alternatives did you consider?
- What was the hardest obstacle?
- Who disagreed, and how did you respond?
- What tradeoff did you make?
- How did you measure success?
- What happened after implementation?
- What would you do differently?

If the answer is vague, ask for one missing detail at a time. If the answer exposes a contradiction, ask neutrally for clarification.

## 4. Scoring Anchors

Score each dimension from 1–5 using the full session, not one isolated answer.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Directly answered | Mostly off-topic or avoids the question | Answers after unnecessary setup | Direct, focused, and complete |
| Structure | Difficult to follow | Understandable with some drift | Clear, concise, easy to follow |
| Individual contribution | Ownership is unclear or inflated | Some personal actions are clear | Decisions and contribution are precise |
| Specificity / credibility | Generic, unsupported, or contradictory | Some concrete detail, some gaps | Specific, internally consistent, credible |
| Result / impact | No outcome or invented implication | Outcome exists but is weakly connected | Confirmed outcome and value are clear |
| Relevance to role | Little connection to role needs | Transferable but indirect | Strong evidence for a priority need |
| Natural delivery | Scripted, jargon-heavy, or rambling | Generally conversational | Concise, confident, authentic speech |

Use scores 2 and 4 for performance between anchors. Do not penalize lack of numbers when a truthful qualitative result is clear.

## 5. End-of-Session Report

Provide:

### Scorecard

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---:|
| Directly answered the question | /5 |
| Structure | /5 |
| Individual contribution | /5 |
| Specificity / credibility | /5 |
| Result / impact | /5 |
| Relevance to the role | /5 |
| Natural delivery | /5 |

### Top 3 strengths

Reference specific behavior or answer moments.

### Top 3 improvements

State exactly what to change. Replace “be more confident” with observable guidance such as “lead with your decision in the first sentence and reduce setup to two sentences.”

### Questions to re-practice

Choose 2–3 questions with the highest improvement value.

### Better answer structure

Restructure weak answers using only facts the candidate already supplied. Mark unresolved blanks rather than filling them.

## 6. Coach Mode

After each answer, keep coaching compact:

1. **What the interviewer heard:** one-sentence interpretation.
2. **What worked:** maximum two points.
3. **Biggest issue:** maximum two issues.
4. **Follow-up question:** continue the interview.

Do not provide a full rewritten answer unless requested. Keep the session moving.
