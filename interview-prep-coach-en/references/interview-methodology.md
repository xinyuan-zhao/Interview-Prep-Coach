Use this reference for analytical outputs and answer development. Keep the user's requested scope: do not run every section for a module-only request.

## 1. Evidence Hierarchy

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. Explicit facts in the candidate's resume or messages.
2. Clarifications confirmed by the candidate.
3. Explicit facts in the JD or company materials supplied by the user.
4. Reliable researched company information, when tools and permission are available.
5. Clearly labeled inference from the JD.

Never present levels 4–5 as candidate experience. Never turn an unknown into a polished claim.

Use an evidence ledger while reasoning:

| Claim | Source | Ownership Confirmed? | Result Confirmed? | Safe to Use? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not display the ledger unless useful, but use it to prevent factual drift.

## 2. Role Analysis

### Role in one sentence

Complete this thought in plain language:

> This role exists to [solve/own/improve] ___ for ___ by ___.

Base it on repeated responsibilities, outcomes, stakeholders, and seniority signals—not the job title alone.

### Competency ranking

Rank 3–5 competencies. For each, capture:

- **JD signal:** quote or close paraphrase of the relevant requirement.
- **Why it matters:** the business or team consequence.
- **How tested:** behavioral evidence, case/problem, technical exercise, resume deep dive, or motivation question.

Separate foundational requirements from differentiators. A frequently repeated responsibility usually ranks above a one-line preference.

### Hiring-problem inference

Infer cautiously from wording such as “build,” “scale,” “improve,” “own,” “standardize,” “partner,” “fast-paced,” or “newly created.” Use calibrated language:

- “The JD suggests…”
- “One likely challenge is…”
- “The hiring manager may be looking for…”

### Interviewer beliefs

Express beliefs as conclusions supported by evidence, for example:

- Can independently diagnose ambiguous problems.
- Communicates tradeoffs clearly to non-technical stakeholders.
- Has delivered a comparable outcome, even in another industry.

## 3. Evidence Mapping

Match at the same level of specificity. “Worked with data” is not automatically strong evidence for “build production SQL pipelines.”

Use these ratings:

- **Strong:** direct, recent, specific evidence with clear ownership and outcome.
- **Medium:** relevant transferable evidence, but smaller scope, indirect context, or incomplete outcome.
- **Weak:** adjacent exposure with limited ownership or specificity.
- **No Evidence:** nothing confirmed yet.

For each weak or missing item, choose an honest strategy:

1. Find a real adjacent example.
2. Explain transferable capability and the difference in context.
3. Show credible learning or preparation already completed.
4. Acknowledge the gap briefly and redirect to stronger evidence.

Never advise the candidate to hide a material gap or claim immediate mastery.

## 4. Story Bank

Select 3–5 stories with high coverage across the top competencies. Favor distinct situations over five versions of the same project.

### Story template

## [Story name]

**Best evidence for:** [competencies]

**Can answer:** [question types]

**S — Situation:** Minimum context needed to understand the problem.

**T — Task:** The candidate's actual responsibility and constraints.

**A — Action:** Personal decisions, sequence, rationale, stakeholders, resistance, alternatives, and tradeoffs.

**R — Result:** Confirmed quantitative or qualitative outcome. State team results as team results.

**L — Learning (optional):** What changed in later behavior or what the candidate would do differently.

**Credibility check:**

- Is “I” versus “we” accurate?
- Is the candidate's contribution observable?
- Are tools and methods confirmed?
- Is the result supported?
- Is causality overstated?
- Which detail still needs confirmation?

**Speaking target:** Usually 60–120 seconds.

### Story coaching

- Compress setup to roughly 10–20% of speaking time.
- Make Action roughly 50% or more when the experience supports it.
- Prefer concrete decision verbs: analyzed, compared, proposed, negotiated, implemented, tested, escalated, simplified.
- Use “we” for shared outcomes and “I” for confirmed individual actions.
- When no metric exists, use observable outcomes: approval obtained, error removed, process adopted, customer issue resolved, deadline met, risk reduced, or stakeholder alignment achieved.

## 5. Question Prioritization

Assign priorities based on evidence, not certainty:

- **High:** central JD need, obvious resume follow-up, motivation question, or material gap.
- **Medium:** useful differentiator or secondary responsibility.
- **Lower:** plausible but weakly supported by the available context.

Keep the list practical. A default plan normally needs 10–15 questions, not 50.

For functional questions, test reasoning at the level implied by the role. Do not generate specialized technical questions when the JD does not support them.

## 6. Critical Answers

### Tell me about yourself

Use four beats:

1. Present professional identity and current focus.
2. One or two relevant past experiences.
3. Two or three strengths demonstrated by evidence.
4. Why this opportunity is the logical next step.

Target 60–90 seconds. Remove biography that does not help role fit.

### Why this role

Combine genuine motivation, capability match, and contribution. Avoid describing only what the candidate hopes to gain.

### Why this company

Use specific evidence such as product, mission, strategy, customers, market, team mandate, or recent credible development. Separate known facts from personal interpretation. If company evidence is unavailable, request it or propose research rather than inventing reasons.

### Why you

Choose three memorable propositions. Use this structure:

> Strength → brief evidence → value for this role.

## 7. Cheat Sheet

Optimize for retrieval under pressure:

- Use keywords, not paragraphs.
- Keep story reminders to triggers and proof points.
- Include risks so the candidate is not surprised.
- Make interviewer questions diagnostic, not performative.
- Keep final reminders behavioral and specific, such as “state your action before the team outcome.”

## 8. Debrief

Capture the interview before memory decays. Separate:

- **Facts:** questions, names, stated priorities, timelines.
- **Candidate interpretation:** what felt strong or weak.
- **Signals:** observations that may have several explanations.
- **Actions:** thank-you content, missing follow-up, story updates, next-round topics.

Do not declare likely success or failure from body language, interview length, or generic closing statements.
